# Race audio: sourcing and implementation brief

Research snapshot: 24 October 2026. Base: `bb6ccd5d90b216423feefef700c2993b1a7eb5a8` (merged PR #80). Branch: `codex/kicklab-race-supercar-audio-research`. This is a research decision, not an asset acquisition or release clearance.

## Finding in the current build

`src/game/19c-race-speed-audio.js` plays a triangle and sine oscillator plus one reusable noise buffer through wind, road, and tire filters. `src/shared/race-speed-perception.js` reduces engine character to one frequency (`58–263 Hz`) and one gain, both driven by normalized RPM and load. A gear change produces a 75 ms triangle tone. Separately, `src/game/03-audio-fx-engine.js` contains a sawtooth `ENGINE`; `src/game/20-race.js` starts it during every running Race step. The 3D renderer updates the other sound in `src/game/21-race-3d.js`, so the two engine voices have separate owners and the layered cue depends on a live 3D render. Canvas fallback receives the generic sawtooth only. Mute, pause, visibility, profile change, and mode exits do not yet converge on one audio lifecycle.

The current RPM is a telemetry proxy (`.18 + speed/top × .56 + gas × .29`), not crankshaft RPM. Drag has four shift bands and a cooldown; other modes have no drivetrain gear state. There are no intake/exhaust load and overrun layers, torque cut/recovery, speed-dependent pitch tied to gear ratio, acceleration grit, or coherent transient design. Broadband road/wind noise can mask the weak engine. These are direct reasons the sound feels smooth and thin instead of raspy and responsive. The requested target is a *designed supercar sensation* for fictional vehicles, not a claim that Kestrel, Cobalt, or Aegis reproduces a named real car.

## Proposed audio contract

Make one Race audio owner consume a small immutable `RaceAudioFrame` from the canonical physics step, at 20–30 Hz. Audio work must not alter gameplay physics or scores. Its fields: `active`, `paused`, `muted`, `vehicleId`, `modeId`, `speedMps`, `engineRpm`, `rpmNormalized`, `throttle`, `deliveredLoad`, `gear`, `shiftPhase`, `brake`, `longitudinalAccel`, `slipAngle`, `driftMode`, `roughness`, `nitroActive`, `heat`, and monotonic event IDs for shift, nitro onset, lift-off and collision. Use the existing normalized telemetry as a temporary input only; a bounded *audio-only* drivetrain model can derive idle/redline RPM and automatic ratios for circuit, hill, drift, endurance, and classic Race. Drag must follow its real four-gear state and shift cooldown. Make a shift torque dip and RPM fall audible without changing the car's physical acceleration.

Signal plan: blend low engine pulse, midrange rasp, intake hiss, exhaust bark, and high-frequency mechanical texture by RPM **and delivered load**; use at least idle/low/mid/high spectral zones and a separate lift-off/overrun response. Filter and crossfade continuously; avoid one loop pitched across the whole range. Shift events cut engine torque, give a short drivetrain clunk, then restore engine energy; downshift blip only when a real downshift occurs. Turbo or supercharger whine is **optional and vehicle-design-specific** after a declared fictional powertrain decision; never imply a real manufacturer. Tire scrub follows slip/grip, road noise follows speed/roughness, wind follows speed squared, and braking adds tire/rotor texture only above a useful speed. NOS has short onset and sustained air/pressure texture only while the game says boost is delivered. Lift-off crackle/backfire is sparse, load-history-gated, and individually volume-capped; no random rapid-fire pops. Drift and skid sound should come from the same slip state, avoiding duplicate `SND.skid()` playback.

Suggested sonic distinction pending listening QA: Kestrel has a quick, bright response and leaner low end; Cobalt has balanced midrange and smoother transitions; Aegis has a lower, denser pulse and more intake/exhaust weight. These are art direction choices, not claims about actual engine configurations. Preserve all five expanded modes and the classic Race path; lower-noise endurance cruising must still communicate throttle and heat. One bus owns engine, drivetrain, tire, road, and event gains, with a limiter and smooth master mute. Build lazily after the user gesture, stop/suspend on pause, hidden page, overlay, result, profile/mode change, and teardown, and recover on resume. Reduced-audio mode should be an explicit user choice: lower continuous noise and transients, disable backfire, keep shift/NOS and critical-heat cues distinguishable, with visible telemetry remaining authoritative. Do not equate reduced motion with reduced audio.

## Bounded prototype and gates

1. **Control and baseline:** instrument frame/event values and capture current audio at idle, launch, steady speed, lift, upshift, brake, drift, NOS, heat, and pause for each vehicle. Include Canvas fallback and Classic/Analog inputs. No network calls or persisted audio data.
2. **Local synthesis:** implement one audio bus behind a default-off prototype flag. Render a 20-second deterministic reference for each vehicle and the same 10-state driving script. Start with native oscillators, noise, filters, gain automation, and a small fixed node budget; use `AudioWorklet` only if profiling shows a need. [Web Audio](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio/) supports scheduled parameters, looped buffers and worklets; [AudioWorklet](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioWorklet) requires a secure context. [Browser autoplay rules](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Best_practices) require a user gesture to start/resume sound.
3. **Listening and device gate:** compare against the baseline on headphones and phone speakers, then measure iPhone Safari and Android Chrome plus desktop, including long Endurance playback, background/foreground, mute/unmute, and 3D context loss. Accept only if no audible clicks, stuck sound, runaway overlap, clipping, or meaningful frame/audio glitch. Set numeric CPU/memory/payload budgets from the measured baseline before adding samples. A provisional sample budget is ≤500 KB compressed for all three cars and ≤8 MB decoded PCM total; these are design targets, not measured facts. Use local FFmpeg for trimming, resampling and loudness analysis if recorded assets are later cleared ([official filters](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html)); choose browser-decoded format via real iOS/Android tests, with a fallback where needed ([codec guide](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Formats/Audio_codecs)).

**Acceptance for an implementation lane:** audible RPM rise/fall with throttle and load at equal speed; each of Kestrel/Cobalt/Aegis is recognizably different in blinded A/B listening; Drag shifts have a coherent torque cut and RPM drop; automatic-mode shifts never contradict visible speed; braking, drift, NOS, lift-off and heat states sound only when their source state occurs; classic and five expanded modes plus Canvas fallback work; mute/pause/visibility/resume are immediate and leak-free; reduced-audio preference and visual cues work; phone and desktop pass the measured performance/payload gate; all asset provenance and redistribution rights are documented; no runtime egress. Do not claim a “real supercar” recording until its provenance and deployment rights are proved.

**Current blocker:** Recorded-library selection is not release-ready because the licence's synchronization clause has not been cleared for separately requested web files. Commissioned recording has no quoted cost or rights agreement. These do not block the zero-cost local synthesis prototype, but they block purchasing, importing, or shipping recorded layers.

## Local synthesis prototype outcome

The default-off `raceAudio=prototype` route now uses self-authored Web Audio only. `src/game/19d-race-supercar-audio.js` owns eight persistent source layers and one dynamics limiter. The Race step passes post-physics speed, telemetry RPM, effective drive gas, gear, braking, slip, boost, heat, and road position into that bus. The audio-only automatic gear envelope covers non-Drag modes; Drag uses its actual four-gear attempt state. Neither audio gearing nor synthesis changes car physics or scoring. The previous oscillator and speed-audio path remains the default. The explicit **Reduced audio** button sits on the visible pre-race card, retains text and `aria-pressed`, and requires no new storage key.

For reproducible review, run `node scripts/render-race-supercar-reference.mjs` to make deterministic 20-second baseline/Kestrel/Cobalt/Aegis WAV comparisons. The WAVs are scripted synthesis references, not recordings of a browser play session. Run `node scripts/qa-race-supercar-audio.mjs http://127.0.0.1:5978/kicklab.html qa-artifacts/race-supercar-audio <commit>` against a local build for the browser report, screenshots, and MP4s. The MP4 audio is captured directly from the running browser audio bus and muxed with the corresponding gameplay video. Generated evidence is ignored by Git and stays local.

On this worktree, `npm run check` passed 223 tests plus web and Pages contract checks. Focused browser QA passed 35 checks: 15 vehicle/mode combinations in Canvas fallback, default-off behavior, canonical Drag shift, pause/resume, mute, pagehide, storage, phone viewport frame pacing, and phone/desktop H.264/AAC walkthroughs. At 390 px, headless Chromium measured 10.0 ms p95 with prototype audio versus 10.2 ms in the baseline run. The phone MP4 audio peak was approximately −13.5 dBFS. These are local browser measurements; physical iPhone Safari and Android Chrome listening, long Endurance playback, and final human sonic judgment remain open gates. No recorded sample rights were exercised, and the focused browser run observed zero external requests.
